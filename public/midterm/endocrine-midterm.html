Three-case rehearsal

Use 20 minutes per case to mimic a 60-minute / 3-case paper. Write first; only then open the model points.

20-MINUTE CASE 1

Case 1 · Polyuria after pituitary surgery

Water balance + pituitary anatomy

A patient develops intense thirst and passes large volumes of very dilute urine after surgery near the pituitary. Plasma osmolality is high. Urine remains dilute during water deprivation but becomes concentrated after desmopressin.
1. Name the most likely diagnosis and subtype.
Show model points

Central diabetes insipidus (central AVP deficiency). The response to desmopressin shows the kidney can respond; the missing signal was AVP.

2. Where is AVP made and where is it released?
Show model points

Synthesised mainly in magnocellular neurons of the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei of the hypothalamus; axons carry it to the posterior pituitary for release into systemic blood.

3. Why is the urine dilute before treatment?
Show model points

Without AVP-V2 signalling, collecting-duct principal cells insert fewer aquaporin-2 water channels, so less water is reabsorbed and urine stays dilute.

4. What does water deprivation test, and what does desmopressin add?
Show model points

Water deprivation asks whether endogenous AVP/concentration can occur. Desmopressin then tests whether the kidney can respond to an AVP analogue, separating central from nephrogenic DI.

5. Which pituitary/anatomy facts could be tested in the same case?
Show model points

Infundibulum connection to the hypothalamus, anterior vs posterior developmental origin, and nearby optic chiasm/third-ventricle relationships.

20-MINUTE CASE 2

Case 2 · Weight loss, goitre and eye signs

Thyroid synthesis + feedback + surgical anatomy

A patient has heat intolerance, tremor, tachycardia, weight loss, a diffuse goitre and exophthalmos. T3/T4 are high, TSH is low, and TSH-receptor-stimulating antibodies are present.
1. What is the diagnosis and why is TSH low?
Show model points

Graves hyperthyroidism. Antibodies stimulate the TSH receptor, raising T3/T4. High thyroid hormone suppresses pituitary TSH by negative feedback, but the antibody keeps stimulating the gland.

2. Describe thyroid hormone synthesis from iodide to T3/T4.
Show model points

Iodide is taken into follicular cells, transferred to colloid, oxidised/organified by TPO onto tyrosines in thyroglobulin, iodotyrosines couple to make T3/T4, and hormone remains stored in colloid until endocytosis/proteolysis releases it.

3. Why does T4 matter if T3 is more potent?
Show model points

T4 is the main thyroid output and circulating prohormone/reservoir. Peripheral deiodinases convert T4 to the more active T3 in tissues.

4. Which nerve is especially vulnerable in thyroid surgery and what deficit follows injury?
Show model points

The recurrent laryngeal nerve, closely related to the gland/inferior thyroid artery region. Injury can produce hoarseness; bilateral injury can severely impair vocal-cord function.

5. What other small endocrine glands must be protected during thyroidectomy?
Show model points

The parathyroid glands, including their blood supply. Loss or devascularisation can cause low PTH and postoperative hypocalcaemia/tetany.

20-MINUTE CASE 3

Case 3 · Stones, bones and high calcium

Parathyroid + calcium + vitamin D

A patient with recurrent renal stones and low bone density has high plasma calcium, high PTH and low-normal phosphate. Imaging suggests a single parathyroid adenoma.
1. What is the diagnosis and why is the PTH inappropriate?
Show model points

Primary hyperparathyroidism. When calcium is high, normal CaSR signalling should suppress PTH; persistent high PTH therefore indicates autonomous parathyroid secretion.

2. Explain the effect of PTH on bone and kidney.
Show model points

PTH promotes calcium mobilisation from bone. In kidney it increases regulated Ca²⁺ reabsorption, reduces phosphate reabsorption (phosphaturia), and stimulates calcitriol formation.

3. How does PTH increase intestinal calcium absorption without acting there as its main direct mechanism?
Show model points

PTH stimulates renal 1α-hydroxylase, increasing calcitriol. Calcitriol acts on intestinal VDR and increases calcium-transport machinery and absorption.

4. Why can phosphate fall while calcium rises?
Show model points

Bone can release both, but PTH makes the kidney excrete phosphate while retaining calcium; net plasma phosphate therefore falls or remains low-normal.

5. What would primary hypoparathyroidism look like instead?
Show model points

Low Ca²⁺, low/inappropriately low PTH and normal-high phosphate; tetany is a classic consequence. After thyroid surgery, parathyroid injury is an important cause.

Cross-lecture cheat sheet

Use this after you have learned the slides, not instead of them.

Pituitary map

  • Hypothalamus surrounds the 3rd-ventricle region; optic chiasm is an anterior landmark.
  • Posterior pituitary = neural extension; anterior = Rathke-pouch/oral ectoderm.
  • Infundibulum = stalk joining pituitary to hypothalamus.

Two hypothalamic routes

  • Magnocellular neurons: AVP/oxytocin → posterior pituitary → systemic blood.
  • Parvocellular neurons: releasing/inhibitory hormones → portal blood → anterior pituitary.

AVP / water

  • ↑ osmolality or ↓ effective volume → AVP ↑.
  • V2 on collecting-duct principal cells → cAMP/PKA → AQP2 insertion → water reabsorption.
  • Central DI responds to desmopressin; nephrogenic DI does not.

Anterior pituitary

  • Somatotroph GH; lactotroph prolactin; thyrotroph TSH; corticotroph ACTH; gonadotroph FSH/LH.
  • Prolactin is normally held down by dopamine.
  • End-organ hormones usually provide negative feedback.

GH / IGF-I

  • GHRH ↑ GH; somatostatin ↓ GH; secretion is pulsatile.
  • GH → liver/tissues → IGF-I; IGF-I mediates much linear growth and feeds back.
  • GH resistance: GH high, IGF-I low; excess before plate closure = gigantism, after = acromegaly.

Thyroid anatomy

  • Two lobes joined by isthmus anterior to upper trachea.
  • RLN is closely related posteriorly and to inferior-thyroid-artery branches.
  • Parathyroids usually lie on posterior thyroid but are variable.

Build thyroid hormone

  • Iodide uptake → TPO oxidation/organification on thyroglobulin → MIT/DIT coupling → T3/T4 stored in colloid.
  • Endocytosis/proteolysis releases T3/T4.
  • T4 is major output; T3 is more potent and is produced partly by peripheral deiodination.

Thyroid lab logic

  • Primary hypo: T3/T4 ↓, TSH ↑.
  • Primary hyper: T3/T4 ↑, TSH ↓.
  • Graves: stimulating TSHR antibodies drive gland despite suppressed pituitary TSH.

Calcium master rule

  • Low ionised Ca²⁺ → CaSR signal ↓ → PTH ↑.
  • PTH: bone Ca²⁺ release ↑; renal Ca²⁺ reabsorption ↑; phosphate reabsorption ↓.
  • PTH → renal 1α-hydroxylase → calcitriol → intestinal Ca²⁺ absorption ↑.

Vitamin D

  • Skin D₃ → liver 25(OH)D (calcidiol) → kidney 1,25(OH)₂D (calcitriol).
  • PTH and low phosphate stimulate renal activation.
  • Calcitriol acts via VDR and increases gut calcium-transport machinery.

Parathyroid lab logic

  • Primary hyperPTH: Ca²⁺ ↑, PTH ↑, phosphate ↓/normal.
  • Primary hypoPTH: Ca²⁺ ↓, PTH ↓, phosphate ↑.
  • PTH resistance: Ca²⁺ ↓ with PTH ↑.

Histology anchors

  • Thyroid: follicles + colloid + follicular cells; C cells make calcitonin.
  • Parathyroid: chief cells make PTH and express CaSR; oxyphil cells are larger/eosinophilic.
  • Anterior pituitary: highly vascular endocrine-cell cords; identify cell/hormone relationships.

Common mistakes worth removing before Friday

  • Do not confuse total calcium with the biologically active free/ionised fraction.
  • Do not say PTH directly increases gut calcium as its main action: it does so mainly through renal calcitriol activation.
  • Do not call osteocytes “mature osteoclasts.” The calcium lecture source contains that typo; osteocytes are osteoblast-lineage cells embedded in bone.
  • Do not diagnose central DI from polyuria alone; use osmolality and the desmopressin response.
  • Do not read TSH alone. Pair it with T3/T4 and decide whether the problem is primary or upstream.
  • Do not treat calcitonin as equally dominant to PTH in normal adult calcium regulation.
  • In a thyroidectomy case, remember both the recurrent laryngeal nerve and parathyroid blood supply.

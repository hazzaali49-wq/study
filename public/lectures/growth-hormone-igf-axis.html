Pulses beat snapshots

Slides 4, 5

GH is released in pulses, with the biggest daily pulse linked to deep sleep. A random sample can therefore be misleading.

  • Regular pulses roughly every 3–4 hours.
  • Sleep timing is part of the physiology, not background detail.
↯ Memory linkThink “night-time wave” rather than “constant level”.

Dual-effector hypothesis

Slides 6

GH acts directly and also makes the liver produce IGF-I. The two signals cooperate in growth while dividing some metabolic jobs.

  • GH is contrainsulin; IGF-I is more insulin-like.
  • Nutrition and insulin tune hepatic IGF-I output.

Length needs an open plate

Slides 7, 8

Chondrocytes multiply, enlarge and are replaced by bone. GH supports differentiation; IGF-I supports proliferation.

  • Plate closure ends linear growth at that site.
  • Bone combines flexible collagen with mineral strength.
＋ Clinical / useful linkA short adult cannot be made taller by GH once the relevant plate is fused.

Metabolic switch

Slides 9, 10

GH favours fatty-acid use and protein synthesis, helping preserve glucose while supporting tissue growth.

  • Lipolysis releases fatty acids and glycerol.
  • IGF-I adds glucose uptake, protein synthesis and insulin sensitivity.
↯ Memory linkGH = “growth plus glucose-sparing”; IGF-I = “growth plus insulin-like uptake”.

Hormone teamwork

Slides 12

Thyroid hormone, insulin and sex steroids make the growth response possible.

  • Low thyroid hormone delays growth.
  • Insulin deficiency can impair growth.

Deficiency is not resistance

Slides 15, 18

In deficiency, GH is missing; in resistance, GH is present but the tissue cannot respond.

  • Congenital hypopituitarism can lower GH and IGF-I together.
  • Laron syndrome gives high GH with low IGF-I.
↯ Memory linkMissing message versus deaf receiver.

Context changes the same output

Slides 20

Malnutrition, inflammation, liver disease and insulin deficiency can all reduce IGF-I, but through different steps.

  • IL-6/TNF-α can disrupt JAK2–STAT5 signalling.
  • Liver disease reduces production capacity.

Adult deficiency is not about height

Slides 21

Without an open growth plate, low GH shows up in muscle, bone, fat and glucose handling.

  • Pituitary tumours, trauma and vascular injury are major causes.
  • Body composition and osteoporosis matter.

Timing sets the phenotype

Slides 22, 23, 24, 25

Before fusion, excess GH lengthens long bones; after fusion, it thickens bone and soft tissue.

  • Gigantism = childhood/pre-pubertal excess.
  • Acromegaly = adult-onset excess, often from a somatotroph adenoma.
↯ Memory linkOpen plate = length; closed plate = thickness.

EXAM CHEAT SHEET

Everything important, compressed

Axis control

  • GHRH stimulates GH; somatostatin inhibits it.
  • Ghrelin, exercise, hypoglycaemia and arginine tend to raise GH.
  • GH and IGF-I provide short- and long-loop negative feedback.

GH versus IGF-I

  • GH: direct growth/metabolic effects; contrainsulin.
  • Liver IGF-I: main endocrine pool; tissues also make local paracrine IGF-I.
  • IGF-I: anabolic, insulin-like effects and feedback inhibition.

Growth plate

  • Epiphysis → resting cartilage → proliferating chondrocytes → hypertrophy → mineralised bone.
  • GH supports differentiation; IGF-I supports proliferation.
  • Plate closure stops linear growth at that site.

Fuel and tissue effects

  • GH promotes lipolysis, fatty-acid transport and protein synthesis.
  • IGF-I increases muscle glucose uptake, protein synthesis and insulin sensitivity.
  • Thyroid hormone is permissive; insulin and sex steroids support the response.

Low GH / low IGF-I

  • Congenital or acquired pituitary damage can cause deficiency.
  • Children: falling growth velocity, central fat, delayed dentition/puberty.
  • Adults: lower lean mass, central adiposity, low bone density and metabolic changes.

High GH / low IGF-I

  • Laron syndrome = GH-receptor resistance.
  • Acquired resistance: malnutrition, inflammation, liver disease, type 1 diabetes, systemic illness.
  • High upstream signal plus low downstream product suggests resistance.

GH excess

  • Before plate fusion: gigantism (length).
  • After fusion: acromegaly (thickness/soft tissue).
  • Pituitary somatotroph adenoma is a classic cause.

Ageing caution

  • Ageing and adult deficiency overlap phenotypically.
  • Overlap does not prove a GH “anti-ageing” treatment.
  • Use evidence and endocrine assessment, not advertising.

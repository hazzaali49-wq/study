T4 is the depot, T3 the accelerator

Slides 10

The thyroid secretes much more T4, while T3 is more potent and is also made peripherally.

  • T4 >90% of direct thyroid output in the slide.
  • T3 is 4–10× more potent.

Most hormone rides a carrier

Slides 13

TBG, transthyretin and albumin bind thyroid hormone in plasma, leaving a tiny free fraction.

  • Free hormone is immediately available to tissues.
  • Carrier affinity and abundance differ.

Activation happens in tissues

Slides 14, 15

Selenium-dependent deiodinases decide whether T4 becomes active T3 or inactive reverse T3/T2.

  • Fasting and cortisol excess reduce activating conversion.
  • The thyroid’s output is not the final tissue signal.

T3 raises baseline throughput

Slides 16, 17

Thyroid hormone increases mitochondrial activity, oxygen use, carbohydrate turnover, lipolysis and LDL clearance.

  • BMR rises in hyperthyroidism and falls in hypothyroidism.
  • Metabolic products cause local vasodilation.

Development has a deadline

Slides 18, 19

Thyroid hormone is permissive for GH and essential for CNS growth, dendrites and myelination.

  • Maternal/fetal deficiency can delay CNS development.
  • Early severe deficiency causes cretinism.

Feedback keeps output steady

Hypothyroidism is a slowdown

Slides 22, 24, 25

Low T3/T4 lowers BMR and cardiac output and causes cold intolerance, lethargy and mood symptoms.

  • Children have additional growth and CNS risk.
  • Goitre can occur when TSH chronically stimulates the gland.

TSH localises the lesion

Slides 23, 26

Low T3/T4 plus high TSH is primary disease; low T3/T4 plus low TSH is secondary disease.

  • Hashimoto antibodies include anti-TPO/anti-thyroglobulin and receptor-blocking antibodies.
  • Levothyroxine replaces missing hormone.

Slides 29, 30, 31

Stimulating autoantibodies bind the TSH receptor, driving hormone production and gland growth even when pituitary TSH is suppressed.

Storm is more than “high thyroid”

Slides 32

Thyroid storm combines extreme hormone excess with fever, tachycardia, arrhythmia, heart failure and dehydration.

  • Beta-blockers reduce sympathetic activation.
  • Glucocorticoids reduce T4→T3 conversion; thionamides block new synthesis.

EXAM CHEAT SHEET

Everything important, compressed

Anatomy and histology

  • Two lobes + isthmus lie in front of the trachea.
  • Follicle centre = colloid; ring = follicular cells; outside = capillaries.
  • C cells make calcitonin, not T3/T4.

Synthesis and storage

  • Iodide + tyrosine on thyroglobulin → iodinated residues.
  • TPO acts at the cell–colloid interface.
  • Colloid stores precursor outside the cell; endocytosis begins release.

Transport and activation

  • T4 is the larger output; T3 is more potent.
  • Most hormone is carrier-bound; free hormone is <0.5%.
  • Outer-ring deiodination makes T3; inner-ring makes reverse T3/T2.

Cell action and metabolism

  • T3 enters cells, binds nuclear receptor/TRE and recruits co-activators.
  • Mitochondria, oxygen use, fuel turnover and LDL clearance rise.
  • BMR is strongly influenced by thyroid hormone.

Growth, CNS and heart

  • Thyroid hormone is permissive for GH and essential for CNS growth/myelination.
  • T3 increases heart rate, contractility and cardiac output.
  • Local vasodilation plus beta-adrenergic sensitivity explains cardiovascular signs.

Hypothyroidism

  • Primary: low T3/T4, high TSH; secondary: low T3/T4, low TSH.
  • Cold intolerance, lethargy, low BMR and bradycardia are the slowdown pattern.
  • Levothyroxine replaces hormone; iodine treats deficiency when relevant.

Hyperthyroidism and Graves

  • Primary: high T3/T4, low TSH; secondary: high T3/T4, high TSH.
  • Graves antibodies stimulate the TSH receptor and can cause goitre/exophthalmos.
  • Thionamides, radioablation or surgery target excess production/tissue.

Thyroid storm

  • Extreme hypermetabolism with fever, tachycardia/arrhythmia, heart failure and dehydration.
  • Beta-blocker + glucocorticoid + thionamide address different parts of the emergency.

Detailed simplified cheat sheet

The master rule

  • Low ionised Ca²⁺ → less CaSR activation → PTH rises.
  • PTH raises Ca²⁺ and lowers phosphate.
  • High Ca²⁺ → CaSR activation → PTH falls.

What PTH does

  • Bone: mobilises Ca²⁺ and phosphate.
  • Kidney: keeps Ca²⁺, wastes phosphate.
  • Kidney: stimulates 1α-hydroxylase → calcitriol.
  • Intestine: indirectly absorbs more Ca²⁺ via calcitriol.

Vitamin D pathway

  • Skin: vitamin D₃.
  • Liver: 25(OH)D / calcidiol.
  • Kidney: 1,25(OH)₂D / calcitriol.
  • PTH and low phosphate stimulate the kidney activation step.

Bone cells

  • Osteoblast = forms bone.
  • Osteoclast = resorbs bone.
  • Osteocyte = mature embedded maintenance cell.

Lab patterns

  • Primary hyperparathyroidism: Ca²⁺ ↑, PTH ↑, phosphate ↓/normal.
  • Primary hypoparathyroidism: Ca²⁺ ↓, PTH ↓, phosphate ↑.
  • Pseudohypoparathyroidism: Ca²⁺ ↓, PTH ↑ because of resistance.

Clinical clues

  • Hypocalcaemia → tetany / overexcitable nerves and muscles.
  • Hypercalcaemia → stones, bone pain, constipation, lethargy, arrhythmia.
  • After thyroid surgery + low Ca²⁺ → think parathyroid injury.

Questions at the end

Short-answer practice

1. Explain how PTH raises plasma calcium but lowers plasma phosphate.

PTH increases calcium/phosphate release from bone, increases renal Ca²⁺ reabsorption, decreases renal phosphate reabsorption, and stimulates calcitriol. The kidney therefore removes phosphate while calcium is retained and absorbed.

2. Trace vitamin D from skin to its active hormone.

7-dehydrocholesterol → vitamin D₃ in skin → 25(OH)D in liver → 1,25(OH)₂D (calcitriol) in kidney via 1α-hydroxylase. PTH and low phosphate stimulate the kidney step.

3. Compare primary hypoparathyroidism with pseudohypoparathyroidism.

Both can give low Ca²⁺ and high phosphate. Primary hypoparathyroidism has low/inappropriately low PTH. Pseudohypoparathyroidism has high PTH because the tissues are resistant to it.

4. Why does hypocalcaemia cause tetany?

Low extracellular Ca²⁺ increases neuronal membrane excitability, including increased sodium permeability, so nerves and muscles fire too easily and involuntary spasms occur.
